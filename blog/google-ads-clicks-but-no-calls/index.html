Google Ads Getting Clicks But No Calls? 10 Things to Check

Google Ads Guide · The Marketing Lab · Updated October 2026

If your Google Ads campaign is receiving clicks but your phone is not ringing, increasing the budget is not automatically the answer. The problem may be the traffic you are buying, the locations you are targeting, the page users land on, or the way calls are being measured.

This checklist is designed for local and service businesses where a phone call, WhatsApp enquiry or lead form is the real goal—not simply website visits.

Start with evidence: before changing bids or budgets, check which search terms, locations, devices and times produced the clicks. A campaign can look active while still attracting the wrong intent.

1. Check the actual search terms

Your keywords tell Google what you want to target, but the Search terms report shows what people actually typed before clicking. This is one of the first places to look when clicks are not becoming enquiries.

Look for searches that are informational, unrelated to your service, outside your service area, looking for jobs, DIY help, free services, parts, or a different type of business. Add clearly irrelevant terms as negative keywords where appropriate.

2. Make sure your keywords show buying intent

Not every relevant keyword has the same commercial value. Someone searching for a definition may be interested in the topic but not ready to call. For a local service business, terms that clearly describe the required service, urgency or location often show stronger intent.

Do not judge a keyword only because it contains your service name. Review the query behind each click and ask whether that person realistically needed what you sell.

3. Review location targeting carefully

A strong ad shown in the wrong area is still wasted traffic. Check campaign location settings, the locations where ads actually appeared, and whether your radius or selected areas match the places you can serve profitably.

This is especially important for recovery, towing, mobile tyre, home-service and emergency businesses where distance directly affects whether a lead can be accepted.

4. Confirm that call and WhatsApp tracking works

Sometimes calls are happening but reporting is incomplete. Test every important contact action yourself: phone buttons, WhatsApp buttons and lead forms. Confirm that your Google Ads or analytics conversion setup fires on the intended action.

Tracking should help you distinguish a campaign that generates no leads from a campaign that generates leads but records them incorrectly.

5. Check the landing page on mobile

Many local-service clicks come from phones. Open the landing page on a real mobile device and check how quickly a visitor can understand the service, service area and next action.

Your phone and WhatsApp buttons should be easy to find. The page should not make an urgent customer scroll through long sections before they can contact you. Slow loading, confusing copy, broken buttons or an unclear service area can reduce enquiries even when the keyword is good.

6. Make the ad and landing page match

If an ad promises one service but sends the visitor to a generic page covering many unrelated services, the user has to work harder to find what they need. Keep the message consistent from keyword to ad to landing page.

For important services, a focused landing page can make the next step clearer: what you do, where you operate, why a customer should choose you, and how to call or message immediately.

7. Review when the clicks happen

Check day and hour performance. If your ads run when nobody can answer the phone, valuable enquiries may be lost. For a genuine 24/7 service, make sure the business can actually respond consistently. For businesses with fixed hours, align advertising and contact expectations with availability.

8. Look at device performance

Phone-call campaigns naturally depend heavily on mobile experience. Compare mobile, desktop and tablet performance. A desktop click may still be valuable, but the path to calling is different. Use device data as a diagnostic signal rather than assuming all clicks behave the same way.

9. Do not change bidding strategy just because of one bad day

A few clicks without a call do not automatically prove that a campaign is broken. Look at enough data to identify a pattern. Frequent major changes can also make performance harder to evaluate.

If you use an automated conversion-focused strategy, accurate conversion tracking is especially important. If the system is optimizing toward the wrong action—or has little useful conversion data—the campaign may not be learning from the outcomes you actually value.

10. Judge lead quality, not only the number of calls

A call is not automatically a good conversion. Track whether enquiries are relevant, within your area, for the correct service and commercially worthwhile. Five poor calls can be less valuable than one genuine customer.

Keep a simple lead log containing the date, source if known, service requested, location, whether the job was accepted and approximate value. Over time, this gives you a much better picture than clicks alone.

A simple order for diagnosing the problem

  1. Review search terms and remove obvious irrelevant intent.
  2. Check the geographic locations receiving traffic.
  3. Test call, WhatsApp and form tracking.
  4. Open the landing page on mobile and test every CTA.
  5. Compare ads, keywords and landing-page message.
  6. Review time, device and lead-quality data.
  7. Only then decide whether bidding, budget or campaign structure needs a larger change.

Clicks are not the final goal

Google Ads can bring people to your business, but a click is only one step. The campaign needs relevant search intent, accurate targeting, a clear offer, a fast contact path and reliable measurement. When those pieces work together, you can make decisions based on leads and customers rather than traffic alone.
